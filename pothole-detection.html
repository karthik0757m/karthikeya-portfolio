Pothole Detection

A machine-learning pipeline that explores pothole detection through simulated smartphone motion and GPS signals.

The question

Could the motion signals recorded during a journey help distinguish ordinary road vibration, speed bumps, and potholes? This project explores that question with a reproducible synthetic dataset.

From signals to features

The pipeline simulates accelerometer, gyroscope, and GPS readings from five users. It applies low-pass Butterworth filtering and sliding-window segmentation, then extracts 48 statistical and signal features per window.

Classification and mapping

Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and SVM classifiers are compared. The documented outputs include model comparisons, confusion matrices, feature-importance plots, and interactive Folium maps of predicted anomalies and severity.

What the results mean

The dataset is simulated, so results do not establish accuracy on real roads or across physical devices. The signal above is an explanatory illustration. The repository documents how a compatible real dataset could replace the synthetic input.
